The Zero-Friction Critical Transition Hypothesis

An Independent Theoretical Exploration of Friction and Its Limiting Behavior

---

Overview

The Zero-Friction Critical Transition Hypothesis is an independent theoretical exploration of friction, its limiting behavior, and the physical conditions under which friction might approach zero.

The central motivation of this work is to examine whether the transition toward a zero-friction state can be described through a consistent physical framework, and to investigate the assumptions, constraints, and implications associated with such a possibility.

This repository provides access to the written hypothesis and serves as a public record of its current formulation and future development.

Research Motivation

Friction plays a fundamental role in classical mechanics and many other areas of physics. It influences motion, energy dissipation, mechanical interactions, and the behavior of physical systems.

Although friction is often treated as an opposing force, its behavior depends on the nature of the interacting surfaces, the surrounding environment, the physical regime, and the model being considered.

This work explores the conceptual question of whether a transition toward zero friction can be meaningfully described under specific physical conditions.

The goal is to formulate the idea clearly, examine its consistency with established physics, and identify questions that could guide further theoretical investigation.

Objectives

The objectives of this hypothesis are:

- To investigate the physical meaning of a zero-friction limit.
- To examine the conditions under which friction may decrease toward zero.
- To explore the relationship between friction, motion, and energy dissipation.
- To identify the assumptions and limitations of the proposed framework.
- To establish a foundation for further mathematical analysis and potential experimental investigation.

These objectives describe the direction of the investigation rather than established conclusions.

Scientific Status

This work is a proposed hypothesis under development. It should not be interpreted as an experimentally verified phenomenon or an established physical theory.

Its scientific validity depends on the clarity of its definitions, the consistency of its mathematical formulation, its compatibility with established physical principles, and the development of testable predictions.

The publication of this document is intended to make the current formulation accessible for discussion, examination, and further development.
